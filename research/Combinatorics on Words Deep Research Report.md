# Combinatorics on Words Deep Research: AA2FR Update (2023–2026)

## Executive summary

The classical minimum-alphabet question for abelian squares is settled: an infinite abelian-square-free word is impossible on three letters and possible on four. Keränen's 1992 construction uses an 85-uniform morphism; four is therefore optimal ([Keränen's construction](https://content.wolfram.com/sites/19/2010/02/Keranen.pdf), [2023 abelian-words survey](https://iris.unipa.it/retrieve/d3c2dbc7-a23f-4242-bd69-2d9952449efe/2207.09937.pdf)). The live AA2FR frontier is not “does a four-letter word exist?” but sharper variants: suppressing all nontrivial abelian squares on three letters (Mäkelä's conjecture), minimizing morphisms and their spectral complexity, deciding abelian-power-freeness of morphic fixed points, and imposing simultaneous avoidance along arithmetic subsequences ([AA2FR explorer](https://joonashuhta.github.io/combinatorics-on-words-research/), [Fici–Puzynina survey](https://iris.unipa.it/retrieve/d3c2dbc7-a23f-4242-bd69-2d9952449efe/2207.09937.pdf)).

The strongest new development is Delépine–Ochem–Rosenfeld (2026): only finitely many coprime pairs \((p,q)\), \(p,q\ge 3\), can fail to admit a ternary infinite square-free word whose \(p\)- and \(q\)-spaced subsequences are also square-free. Their proof combines multi-valued and circular square-free morphisms and introduces square-free transducers ([arXiv:2605.29853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29853)). This directly validates arithmetic-subsequence search as a major active direction, but it concerns ordinary squares, not abelian squares.

Computationally, the field's strongest established methods are morphism search plus finite test criteria, exhaustive/backtracking verification, automata-based theorem proving with Walnut, and proof-assistant formalization in Isabelle/HOL. SAT is a promising encoding layer, especially for finite avoidance or morphism synthesis, but the reviewed core papers more often use custom backtracking, finite automata, or direct exhaustive C/C++ checks than named SAT solvers ([Walnut](https://walnut-theorem-prover.github.io/), [Isabelle AFP entry](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Combinatorics_Words.html), [2026 arithmetic-progression paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.29853)).

### Named foundational researchers in the request

- **Veikko Keränen** solved Erdős's four-letter abelian-square problem using the 85-uniform morphism and later continued computer-aided searches for alternative constructions ([Keränen overview](https://content.wolfram.com/sites/19/2010/02/Keranen.pdf)).
- **R. C. Entringer, D. E. Jackson, and J. A. Schatz** are a foundational historical trio. Their 1974 *On non-repetitive sequences*, JCTA 16, 159–164, established key binary repetition bounds and posed a conjecture later refuted in the cubefree setting by Rampersad–Shallit–Wang ([later resolution](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0302303)). They are not a current AA2FR research group.
- **Maxime Crochemore** and **Lucian Ilie** are central to string algorithms, repetitions, factorization and the algorithmic side of combinatorics on words; their work is more directly relevant to scalable detection and indexing than to current abelian-square construction ([Crochemore–Ilie–Rytter survey](https://www.mimuw.edu.pl/~rytter/MYPAPERS/Crochemore-Ilie-Rytter.pdf)).
- **Jean-Jacques Pansiot** is foundational for decidability of periodicity of morphic words; the 2026 abelian-periodicity paper explicitly builds on the Harju–Linna–Pansiot classical line ([1986 periodicity paper](https://www.numdam.org/item/ITA_1986__20_1_43_0.pdf), [2026 extension](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30306)).
- **F. M. Dekking** is foundational for substitutions/morphic words and abelian properties. This is primarily a historical/theoretical lineage rather than the most active 2023–2026 AA2FR publication cluster ([Delft repository record](https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_0df14eed-36d7-4cfd-84be-569d996eb561)).
- **Michaël Rao** should be added to the watchlist: computer-assisted pattern avoidance, Dejean's conjecture, \(k\)-abelian powers, and the Rao–Rosenfeld decision method already implemented in AA2FR make his work unusually close to the project ([Rao publications](https://perso.ens-lyon.fr/michael.rao/publi.php?lang=en)).

### SAT/SMT assessment

A direct SAT model can use one-hot variables \(x_{i,a}\) for the letter at position \(i\). Ordinary square avoidance becomes disequality constraints between adjacent blocks. Abelian-square avoidance is harder because each forbidden pair requires proving that at least one letter count differs; practical encodings use cardinality networks, pseudo-Boolean constraints, or an SMT solver with integer sums. Incremental solving can extend \(n\), retain learned clauses, and emit DRAT/LRAT certificates for unsatisfiable maximum-length claims. Generic SAT methodology supplies certificate-producing finite combinatorial search ([SAT overview](https://cs.curtisbright.com/talks/canadam-slides.pdf)); modern string solvers also combine regular constraints, equality, bounded length and incremental SAT ([string-constraint paper](https://assets.amazon.science/be/e6/c0265a14477ca4558784008404ea/solving-string-constraints-using-sat.pdf)).

However, no reviewed 2023–2026 core AA2FR paper identifies MiniSat, Glucose, Kissat or CaDiCaL as its primary engine. Claims should therefore say “SAT is a recommended computational approach,” not “SAT is standard in abelian-square research.” For this project, **Kissat/CaDiCaL + proof logging**, or **OR-Tools CP-SAT** for rapid modeling, would be sensible experiments; results should be checked by an independent verifier and compared with the existing DFS.

## 4. Open problems and conjectures

### Highest relevance to AA2FR

1. **Mäkelä's ternary conjecture.** Does an infinite ternary word exist whose only abelian squares are \(00,11,22\)? This is Conjecture 20 in the 2023 survey and is the project's central \(K=2\ldots5\) seam problem after applying the Rao–Rosenfeld large-period construction ([survey](https://iris.unipa.it/retrieve/d3c2dbc7-a23f-4242-bd69-2d9952449efe/2207.09937.pdf), [AA2FR explorer](https://joonashuhta.github.io/combinatorics-on-words-research/)).
2. **Decision problem for morphic fixed points.** Given a substitution \(h\) and \(k\ge2\), decide whether its fixed point is abelian \(k\)-power-free. The survey records this as open in general, together with the conjecture that Carpi's sufficient preservation conditions are also necessary ([Fici–Puzynina](https://iris.unipa.it/retrieve/d3c2dbc7-a23f-4242-bd69-2d9952449efe/2207.09937.pdf)).
3. **Binary finite-abelian-cube problem.** Is there an infinite binary word containing only finitely many abelian cubes? This remains explicitly open in the survey ([Fici–Puzynina](https://iris.unipa.it/retrieve/d3c2dbc7-a23f-4242-bd69-2d9952449efe/2207.09937.pdf)).
4. **Spectrally simple abelian-square-free morphisms.** The AA2FR question asking for a four- or five-letter abelian-square-free morphic word with only three eigenvalues of modulus at least 1 is a legitimate project-specific open problem, but not yet a standard published conjecture ([AA2FR explorer](https://joonashuhta.github.io/combinatorics-on-words-research/)).
5. **Growth/enumeration.** The survey conjectures \(O(n^{3/2})\) nonequivalent abelian-square factors in a length-\(n\) word and conjectures every binary length-\(n\) word contains at least \(\lfloor n/4\rfloor\) distinct abelian-square factors ([Fici–Puzynina](https://iris.unipa.it/retrieve/d3c2dbc7-a23f-4242-bd69-2d9952449efe/2207.09937.pdf)). These are separate from counting abelian-square-free words and should not be conflated with the explorer's growth notation.

### Arithmetic and number-theoretic directions

- **The \((5,8)\) pair:** For ordinary ternary square-free words, the existence of a word simultaneously square-free modulo 5 and 8 remains open and is conjectured negative ([Delépine–Ochem–Rosenfeld](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.29853)).
- **General pair threshold:** The authors conjecture positivity for every coprime \(p,q\ge3\) with \(p+q\ge14\); they also ask for shifted residue classes, \(k\)-tuples of moduli, infinite increasing modulus sequences, all residue classes, and exact exponential growth rates of the languages \(CL_{p,q}\) ([Delépine–Ochem–Rosenfeld](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.29853)).
- **Abelian antipower dichotomy:** Does every infinite word contain abelian powers of every order or abelian antipowers of every order? This is Problem 14 in the recent abelian survey ([Fici–Puzynina](https://iris.unipa.it/retrieve/d3c2dbc7-a23f-4242-bd69-2d9952449efe/2207.09937.pdf)).
- **Abelian-bordered versus abelian-periodic:** If all sufficiently long factors are abelian bordered, must the infinite word be abelian periodic? This is recorded as Problem 42 in the survey ([Fici–Puzynina](https://iris.unipa.it/retrieve/d3c2dbc7-a23f-4242-bd69-2d9952449efe/2207.09937.pdf)).

## 5. Recommended AA2FR research program

1. **Separate three theorem layers.** Maintain distinct test suites and terminology for ordinary square-free, fully abelian-square-free, and “only trivial period-1 abelian squares.” The [current explorer](https://joonashuhta.github.io/combinatorics-on-words-research/) already documents past failures caused by mixing these layers.
2. **Turn every finite obstruction into a certificate.** Add SAT/CP-SAT as an independent engine for bounded \(K=2\ldots5\) searches, with proof logs or a compact witness checker. Preserve symmetry-breaking assumptions in the certificate metadata.
3. **Replace prefix evidence with template closure.** Generalize the Rao–Rosenfeld/ancestor method so a candidate morphism is certified by a finite closed set of Parikh-difference templates, not by scanning \(N\) letters.
4. **Search morphisms and transducers together.** The 2026 square-free-transducer concept broadens the design space beyond one fixed image per letter ([arXiv:2605.29853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29853)). Multi-valued images may provide seam flexibility missing from uniform cyclic morphisms.
5. **Build an automatic-sequence export.** If a candidate admits an automatic or Pisot-substitutive presentation, generate Walnut predicates and automata for period/Parikh constraints, following the effective constructions in [Couvreur et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13584) and [Popoli–Shallit–Stipulanti](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.02409.pdf).
6. **Formalize the reusable core.** Contribute Parikh vectors, abelian equivalence, abelian borders and abelian powers to the [Isabelle AFP combinatorics-on-words development](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Combinatorics_Words.html), then connect finite generated certificates to an infinite fixed-point theorem.
7. **Track the right people and venues.** Monitor Currie, Rampersad, Shallit, Ochem, Rosenfeld, Puzynina, Fici, Rao, Mol, Peltomäki, Stipulanti, Popoli, and the WORDS/CPM/DLT conference streams. The [WORDS 2025 program](https://words2025.loria.fr/en/program/) provides the best compact snapshot of the active international network.

## Bottom line

AA2FR is correctly positioned on a genuine open frontier: ternary suppression of all nontrivial abelian squares, not the already-solved four-letter existence problem. The most promising path is a hybrid of morphism/transducer synthesis, independent SAT-backed finite obstruction search, finite Parikh-template certification, and eventual Isabelle/Walnut mechanization. The 2026 arithmetic-progression work adds both a fresh theoretical object—square-free transducers—and a concrete model for publishing exhaustive verification code alongside structural proofs ([Delépine–Ochem–Rosenfeld](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.29853)).
