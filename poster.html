Definition

An abelian square is a factor uv of a word in which the two halves have equal length — the half-length K — and v is an anagram of u: the two halves carry identical Parikh vectors. An ordinary square is the special case v = u.

Over the ternary alphabet {a, b, c} the single-letter squares aa, bb, cc are abelian squares of the smallest possible half-length, and no infinite word can avoid them. The aa2f condition asks for everything else to be avoided: a word is aa2f when it contains no abelian square of half-length K ≥ 2 at any position. The stricter aa2fr condition additionally forbids the six FORBID4 factors (row 9).

The condition is a global one. It cannot be packed into a bounded memory window, and that single fact is responsible for most of what has failed in this project.

Central question
Mäkelä's conjecture — open

There exists an infinite ternary word whose only abelian squares are the single-letter squares. Equivalently: an infinite aa2f word exists.

Stated as a conjecture in the survey literature and as a problem in the source paper; both formulations opened and compared word for word (row 4, PRIMARY). Open for the half-lengths K = 2…5.

The large half-lengths are settled: an infinite ternary word avoiding every abelian square of period greater than five is known to exist (row 7, PRIMARY), obtained by projecting the fixed point of a morphism over a larger alphabet (rows 5 and 6a). Exactly the small half-lengths remain.

Why that gap and no other (row 7b). The decision procedure behind the positive result decides abelian-power avoidance for pure morphic words at all periods, but for morphic images only at large periods, and only under spectral hypotheses on the incidence matrices. The ternary word is available only as such an image. Decidability at small periods is lost precisely in the projection.

An equivalence worth knowing (row 50). The conjecture is equivalent to the statement that aa2f words exist at every length. Any nonzero lower bound on the growth rate would therefore prove it outright: narrowing the growth from below is not a partial goal, it is the entire problem. Narrowing it from above (row 33) is genuine partial progress.

Methods

Exhaustive sweeps. Uniform ternary morphisms are enumerated up to a declared block length and each candidate fixed point is scanned until it breaks; uniform maps from the larger alphabet onto ternary blocks are swept the same way. A violation found in a prefix of a fixed point is final; the absence of one is evidence bounded by the window that was scanned, and is always reported with that window.

Container and SFT analysis. The constraint over the open half-lengths fits in a finite window, so it can be represented as a shift of finite type over de Bruijn states. Its strongly connected structure gives necessary conditions on every possible witness — letter frequency intervals from Karp's cycle mean, each bound re-verified independently by Bellman–Ford, plus exact counts of container words. Necessary, never sufficient.

Rauzy graphs and factor complexity. Branching structure, special factors, Cassaigne's second-difference formula as an independent cross-check, dead-end factors as an order-independent formulation of where a constraint bites, and factor counts feeding the Fekete bound.

Decision procedures. The template and ancestor machinery is re-derived inside the project with the authors' own apparatus — a re-derivation, explicitly not an independent proof — together with Smith normal form, Jordan structure and eigenvalue bounds for the hypotheses those procedures require.

Search engineering. Extension-depth tables as provably sound pruning oracles, resumable runs, affine transport of tables between equivalent alphabets, and positive and negative controls inside every module: a module throws rather than return an answer it cannot justify.

What is closed for good

These are not estimates. Each is a finite computation whose search tree was emptied, and each holds only inside its declared window. “Does not cover longer block lengths” or “does not cover larger alphabets” is not a footnote — it is part of the claim.

Negative results are results

The graveyard is a first-class part of this laboratory, and its threshold for entry is deliberately low. It records dead ends, but also ideas that worked and did not pay, ideas that worked in the wrong place, and a working method that turned out to be wrong even though its output was flawless. A dead end is not a mistake; it is measured knowledge about a direction not worth taking. Every entry below was measured, not guessed, and each keeps a permanent number because the rest of the repository points at it.

§12An ordering heuristic transplanted into the aa2f search. Dramatic in its own setting, a loss here: the technique is specific to the setting, not to the search.
§11A search-engine snippet used as a source. The snippet returned a literal claim complete with an author name and a numeric bound; neither was in the original. Methodological dead end, and the reason a whole attribution now stands under a use ban.
§10A definition-level verifier as an independent checker. It ran flawlessly and never reached its own target. The axis of independence was the wrong one — a correct answer to a question nobody needed answered.
§9Net gain of a pruning table within a single run. None: building the table costs about one search. The value is exclusively in reuse.
§8The extension table as a record-hunt accelerator. The same longest word, pruned and unpruned. Branch-and-bound does not bite when the incumbent best keeps improving.
§7Container relaxation as an elimination tool for additive squares. It did not die at any reachable window size, while exhaustive search on the real language finished in seconds. Elimination is a search question, not a container question.
§6A filter distilled from the record word's own factors. It would discard the large majority of perfectly legal continuations, purely because one search never happened to visit them. Overfitting: a ceiling, not a springboard.
§5FORBID4 as a universally lethal rule. Those factors occur 2 820 times (row 41) in the verified record word. Banning them globally would have made the record mathematically impossible to find.
§4“A morphism keeps the Parikh imbalance small.” Measured to be the opposite, and wrong in both directions: the morphic word is the more unbalanced one, because a transfer eigenvalue off the unit circle drives the imbalance up without bound.
§3Reverse-engineering the record word into a morphism. Its factor complexity follows the language rather than the linear bound a morphic word must obey. The rule cannot be extracted because there is no rule: the word is the product of a well-pruned depth-first walk.
§2A strongly connected component of a Rauzy graph as a proof of infiniteness. The abelian condition is global; a component is local. An excellent prefilter with exactly zero proof value — and the reason the container results above are phrased as necessary conditions only.
§1Scanning uniform morphisms at ever larger block lengths. The maximum surviving length tracks the logarithm of the sample size. The growth appears even if the mathematics is doing nothing at all, so a longer scan buys no structural signal.

Rejected on structural grounds, without a run: hyperbolic Parikh space (it destroys additivity), the spectral gap as a predictor of a morphism's correctness (the incidence matrix forgets order), and a translation to a general solver before a finite criterion exists (the translation is the hard part). Separately quarantined: framings that measure the implementation rather than the mathematics — search-tree geometry, phase transitions in search, survival curves. Those may be measured; their outputs may never be presented as properties of the language.

How a claim earns its status
Stated
A claim enters the ledger with a source and a date, never as an assertion in prose alone.
→
Questioned
A drift checker rejects overclaiming language, dangling references and rotted documents. It has caught its own author more than once.
→
Measured
The computation is run, with positive and negative controls and, where possible, two independent code paths.
→
Corrected
Theorem numbers, attributions and provenance are fixed in place, and the correction is written into the row rather than over it.
→
Verified
Only when somebody has opened the primary source and compared it word for word does a row become PRIMARY.

A retracted claim is never deleted. It stays visible in REJECTED state, with its reasons attached, so that nobody can quietly put it back. A morphism's supposed origin in a sporadic group was withdrawn for lack of any support (row 8); an attribution of an implementation was withdrawn as unaudited (row 14); an audit found that a mode advertised as certifying a construction never loaded the construction at all and printed a hard-coded zero (row 26); a theorem re-derivation was withdrawn as unverified and only later done properly, in a new row (row 43).

And a retraction can itself be overturned. The journal reference for the construction's source paper was retracted because nobody had traced it — the preprint record carried no journal field. It was later restored when an independent bibliography corroborated it character by character: same authors, same title, same volume, issue, pages and year (row 23). The ground for retraction had been “not traced”, not “wrong”, and that is exactly why it was recoverable. The row still sits at the weaker status, because a third-party bibliography is not the publisher's own record.

A finite check is always reported with its window, never with the words proven or certified unless a stated bound sits beside them.
